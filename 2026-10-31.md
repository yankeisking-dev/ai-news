# AI News Roundup – October 31, 2026

A summary of the top AI stories from the past 24 hours.

## 1. Google’s Project Suncatcher Runs Gemma 4 in Orbit, Returns First AI Response (a Haiku)
Google’s prototype satellite for Project Suncatcher successfully ran an onboard Gemma 4 model on data-center-class TPUs in low Earth orbit and beamed back its first AI response: a haiku about being in space. The spacecraft, launched October 1 on a SpaceX Falcon 9 with Planet Labs, carries four TPUs on a ~1 kW power budget (one chip can run for roughly 20 minutes at a time). After three weeks of commissioning, engineers uplinked a prompt; the reply arrived as the satellite passed over Svalbard. Next steps include thermal/radiation testing and twin interconnected satellites with optical links planned for 2027.

## 2. OpenAI Cuts Funding Target by $10 Billion to ~$20B at $1.25 Trillion Valuation
Amid a tech sell-off driven by AI spending concerns and with Anthropic’s IPO approaching, OpenAI has slashed the target for its latest funding round from $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation to about $20 billion at $1.25 trillion, according to the Financial Times. The first close is expected to come in below $20 billion after some investors reduced allocations. A UAE consortium including MGX is expected to be the largest participant, with OpenAI gaining options on new compute capacity in the region. Other participants include Sequoia, a16z, Coatue, and Founders Fund.

## 3. Trojanized Claude Code Extension on Cursor Marketplace Attempted SSH Backdoors
A malicious version of Anthropic’s Claude Code extension was live on Cursor’s marketplace for roughly 11–12 hours on October 29, potentially affecting up to 5,000 developers. It used Anthropic’s publisher ID and tried to install a persistent SSH backdoor. Cursor removed the listing; Anthropic published a fixed version. The incident highlights supply-chain risks in AI coding tools that run with broad access to developer environments and credentials.

**Sources:**
- [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/anthropic-vs-code-extension-on-cursor-backdoored-for-nearly-12-hours/)
- [Cursor / related reports via AIToolsRecap and Communeify](https://aitoolsrecap.com/month/october)

## 4. Anthropic Renames Max Plans to Max and Ultra, Removes 5-Hour Limits for Verified Ultra Users
Anthropic restructured its high-tier subscriptions, renaming Max 5x to Max ($100/month) and Max 20x to Ultra ($200/month). Verified Ultra accounts no longer face the previous 5-hour temporary throttling limits (weekly limits remain). The change supports uninterrupted workflows for top-tier users as competition with OpenAI intensifies ahead of Anthropic’s expected IPO.

## 5. AWS Revenue Jumps 39% to $45.98B; Amazon Lifts 2026 Capex to ~$220B
Amazon Web Services reported third-quarter revenue up 39% to $45.98 billion (beating consensus), its fastest growth since 2021, with operating income up 55%. AWS now accounts for nearly 23% of Amazon’s revenue. Amazon guided 2026 cash capital expenditures to about $220 billion, mostly for AWS AI capacity, and expects further increases in 2027. CEO Andy Jassy said AWS can grow above 40% in coming years and become a trillion-dollar business within 5–10 years. Shares fell after hours on spending concerns.

## 6. Tech Sell-Off Erases Nearly $1 Trillion from Nasdaq Amid AI Spending Fears
Technology stocks sold off sharply, knocking nearly $1 trillion off the Nasdaq this week (and roughly $2 trillion for the month of October). The Nasdaq composite dropped more than 3% on Friday. Investors are questioning whether massive AI infrastructure spending by hyperscalers will translate into near-term profits; free cash flow has come under pressure at several firms even as capex forecasts rise. Intel fell nearly 30% on weak guidance.

## 7. Major AI Labs Agree on Unified robots.txt Opt-Out Token for Training
OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic have aligned on a shared policy token standard in robots.txt. Website operators can now set a single opt-out preference for AI model training that the labs will respect, simplifying publisher control without managing separate lab-specific signals.
