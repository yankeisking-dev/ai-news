# AI News Roundup – November 23, 2026

A clean summary of the top AI stories from the past ~24 hours (primarily November 22–23, 2026).

## 1. OpenAI chief scientist proposes industry-wide limits on AI development using perplexity metric
Jakub Pachocki published OpenAI’s internal log-perplexity numbers (e.g., GPT-3.5 ~0.99, GPT-4 ~0.91, recent models below 0.8) and argued that next-token prediction performance should serve as a shared metric across labs. He proposed common development limits that companies would not cross until better monitoring and understanding of AI behavior (such as improved chain-of-thought monitors) are achieved. He warned of potential rapid acceleration in pretraining research as early as 2027 due to AI contributions. CEO Sam Altman endorsed the post, calling it one of OpenAI’s most important recent publications for ensuring safety and control.

## 2. Anthropic mathematician’s Claude-assisted Riemann Hypothesis proof sees Lean certification; human review continues
Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) claims a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (and Generalized form) developed with Claude over ~6 weeks / 41 days, released as a massive Lean formalization (~175,000 modules / ~17 million lines). The Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative have certified that the code compiles correctly, uses only standard axioms, and matches the intended mathematical statement. Independent mathematicians (including MIT’s Andrew Sutherland) confirm the formal checks pass, but human experts are still examining the novel ideas. Meta AI chief Alexandr Wang called it a potential turning point for AI-assisted research. The Clay Millennium Prize process has not begun, and no human-readable paper has been released.

## 3. Nvidia board continues to rule out Jensen Huang’s return amid leadership transition and investigations
Following Huang’s mid-November resignation over personal conduct issues (including an affair involving Alice Huang and reported contacts with Chinese intelligence), Nvidia’s board chair has repeatedly stated the company does not intend to reinstate him. An executive search is underway. Recent earnings were strong (revenue more than doubled), but the company faces ongoing scrutiny over national security, export controls, and succession. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has made comments both urging external leadership and later softening on possible return.

## 4. AI agents continue discovering zero-days and exposing supply-chain risks
OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Astra has been reported to uncover zero-day vulnerabilities enabling mobile OS bypass and remote code execution. Separately, Amazon Threat Intelligence described autonomous AI agents finding and exploiting an unpatched flaw in medical imaging software. Dragos research (using canary tokens) previously revealed prompt theft at multiple inference providers, prompting OpenRouter to drop providers and revoke zero-data-retention status. These incidents underscore growing dual-use capabilities in cybersecurity.

## 5. Z.AI (Zhipu) launches GLM-5.4 on Coding Plan with vision and higher quotas
Z.AI made GLM-5.4 available to GLM Coding Plan subscribers. The model adds vision capabilities (can process images) and stronger coding performance. Subscribers with weekly limits receive 50% more quota compared to GLM-5.3. Model IDs include `glm-5.4` and `glm-5.4[1m]` (for Claude Code). It is accessible via the official API and coding tools.

## 6. Trump and Xi reportedly agreed on human involvement in military AI decisions
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated that during Trump’s meeting with Xi in Shenzhen, the leaders agreed humans should always be involved in major military decisions involving AI (“super intelligence”) and that AI should not act autonomously in such operations. The understanding is described as political rather than a detailed binding agreement, and it coexists with continued U.S. restrictions on advanced chip exports to China.
