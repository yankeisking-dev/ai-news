# AI News Roundup — November 24, 2026

Clean summary of the top AI stories from the past ~24 hours (primarily Nov 23–24, 2026).

## 1. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO
Nvidia’s board unanimously voted on November 23 to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO, and board director, effective immediately. Huang had resigned on November 12 amid reports of affairs (including one with a businesswoman U.S. authorities suspected of spying for China). The board cited input from senior U.S. government officials and concluded his return poses no national security risk; President Trump publicly supported the decision. An independent investigation continues. Nvidia shares jumped sharply on the news.

## 2. Anthropic launches Claude Ship
Anthropic introduced Claude Ship (beta) on November 23 for Claude Team and Enterprise plans with GitHub repositories. It bundles agents that review pull requests, push fixes for issues like failing checks or conflicts, and (via Claude Approvals) can auto-approve routine changes based on a team-written policy file. Developers can also use a `/ship` command in Claude Code. The goal is to let teams ship code as fast as they (or agents) write it while keeping humans in control for higher-risk changes.

## 3. Google DeepMind agents discover faster quantum algorithms (formally verified)
Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI used teams of Gemini 4 agents, coordinated via a shared wiki and periodic virtual “conferences,” to design more efficient quantum circuits. Results were formally verified in the Lean theorem prover (necessary because the circuits are too large to simulate classically). Reported gains include ~11.8× lower cost for an inertial-confinement-fusion simulation, ~8× for a photochemical molecule, ~2.5× for a drug-metabolism enzyme, and a 290× improvement on an arcsin arithmetic routine.

Sources:
- [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/finding-faster-quantum-algorithms-with-agents-and-formal-verification/)
- [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09822v1)

## 4. Amazon acquires Decart’s AI optimization technology (~$7.5B)
Amazon announced it is purchasing the Decart Optimization Stack technology and hiring Decart’s co-founders and research/engineering team. The deal is reported at about $7.5 billion (cash + stock, performance-tied over five years). The software helps AI models run more efficiently across chips and is aimed at improving Amazon’s Trainium/Inferentia performance to better compete with Nvidia. Amazon is not acquiring Decart’s own models (e.g., Lucy or Oasis).

## 5. Meta launches Muse personal AI agent in the UK
Meta rolled out its Muse personal AI agent to UK adults (18+) on iOS, Android, muse.ai, and WhatsApp. Muse is designed to complete multi-step tasks (e.g., Christmas shopping, energy deals, calendars, travel) rather than just chat. It asks for approval before sensitive actions, does not see passwords or card details, and partners with UK retailers/services including Marks & Spencer, Sainsbury’s, ASOS, Currys, and easyJet. Free with optional paid tiers.

## 6. OpenAI rolls out chat-tuned GPT-6.2 Sol to ChatGPT
OpenAI began rolling out a version of GPT-6.2 Sol optimized for the Chat tab in ChatGPT to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users worldwide. Improvements include better handling of complex instructions, finer image understanding, and more useful web-search follow-ups (e.g., price-drop alerts). GPT-5.6 Sol and Terra are scheduled to retire from relevant surfaces on December 23. (GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol had already launched in Work and Codex the prior week.)
