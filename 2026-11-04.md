# AI News Roundup — November 4, 2026

Clean summary of the top AI stories from the past ~24 hours (as of early November 4, 2026 IST).

## Top Stories

1. **Google will disable Search for K-12 schools that reject AI tools**  
   Google is turning off Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search by default for K-12 student accounts starting early 2027. Schools that do not explicitly opt in to AI will also lose student access to Search, because the company says "Search and AI are deeply integrated." The policy has drawn sharp criticism from educators, parents, and politicians (including calls of monopoly pressure or "extortion"), with Google clarifying that schools can enable services individually but Search retains built-in AI features.  
   - [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025411/google-turn-off-search-ai-features)  
   - [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/03/tech/google-ai-search-policy)  
   - [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/google-responds-to-outrage-over-removing-search-in-schools-that-ban-ai.html)

2. **Oracle abandons ~$7B five-year AI chip lease deal with Tencent under US pressure**  
   Oracle has canceled a deal that would have given Tencent access to roughly 100,000 advanced AI chips in Southeast Asian data centers (Tencent’s largest overseas lease). The Financial Times reports the cancellation followed concerns raised by Trump administration officials; Chinese officials also urged Tencent to support domestic chips. Reuters could not immediately verify.  
   - [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/oracle-abandons-7-billion-tencent-ai-deal-under-trump-pressure-ft-reports-2026-11-04/)  
   - [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/oracle-scraps-7-billion-deal-to-lease-chips-to-tencent-ft-says)

3. **Nvidia tells cloud and data-center partners: at least 51% of services to non-Chinese customers**  
   Nvidia has verbally instructed cloud computing clients worldwide (especially in Asia) that at least 51% of new business must go to non-Chinese firms, or they risk losing access to its chips. Existing contracts are exempt. The move targets Chinese firms renting capacity overseas to bypass export controls; enforcement includes inspections and audits. Nvidia has not confirmed in writing.  
   - [Financial Times (via multiple outlets)](https://www.ft.com/)  
   - [Crypto Briefing summary](https://cryptobriefing.com/nvidia-cloud-clients-51-percent-non-chinese/)  
   - [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/11/62808888/nvidia-tightens-the-screws-on-chinas-ai-access-cloud-partners-worldwide-must-restrict-chinese-customers-under-new-revenue-rule-report)

4. **OpenAI partners with Cathedral Systems (ex-DOGE team that accessed Anthropic) to test its security**  
   OpenAI announced a partnership with Cathedral, the cybersecurity startup founded by former DOGE staffers whose AI agents reportedly reached Anthropic’s business systems (HR, sales, Slack) in under 12 hours via third-party software during an authorized test. Anthropic disputed the severity and said model weights and conversations were untouched. OpenAI is also quadrupling bug-bounty rewards amid reports of years of internal security warnings.  
   - [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/openai-hires-doge-alumni-who-hacked-into-anthropic-to-strengthen-security-2000834432)  
   - [Washington Post (original Cathedral/Anthropic report)](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/26/researchers-cybersecurity-start-up-cathedral-got-inside-anthropic/)

5. **Anthropic open-sources FlashProve for cryptographic verification of AI model outputs**  
   FlashProve is a zkSNARK-based prototype that lets anyone check a response came from a specific committed set of model weights without revealing the weights. Tested on a 400B MoE model (Llama 4 Maverick-like) at ~110 tokens/sec on one H100, with ~19 MB proofs verifiable on CPU in ~17 seconds. Anthropic notes Claude helped improve the system; it addresses router substitution and trust issues. Separately, Anthropic’s S-1 filing has highlighted high compute costs (58% of revenue) and progress toward recursive self-improvement.  
   - [Anthropic research / GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve)  
   - [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/04/anthropic-flashprove-provable-inference/)  
   - [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-flashprove-provable-inference-zksnark/)

6. **AMD posts largest and most profitable quarter ever, driven by AI/data-center demand**  
   AMD reported record Q3 2026 revenue of $13.4 billion (up sharply year-over-year), with data-center revenue nearly doubling. CEO Lisa Su called it the largest and most profitable quarter in company history; the firm guided even higher for Q4. Deals with OpenAI and Meta contributed, though they also triggered a $1B quarterly charge related to warrants.  
   - [Coverage via TechInsights / earnings](https://techinsights.app/signal)  
   - [Related Economic Times note on deals](https://m.economictimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence)

7. **Sam Altman: Human control must constrain progress toward superintelligence**  
   OpenAI CEO Sam Altman stated that people should always remain in control of the future and should not cede decision-making to superintelligence. He argued that the requirement of human control should ultimately constrain AI progress, rejecting the idea of humans being relegated to tasks that AI systems "think make sense to reward."  
   - [Economic Times / reporting on Altman statement](https://m.economictimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence)  
   - [X post by @sama](https://x.com/sama)

These stories highlight intensifying US-China AI chip and compute controls (Nvidia, Oracle/Tencent), education and monopoly friction around AI deployment (Google), security and verification challenges at the frontier labs (OpenAI/Cathedral, Anthropic FlashProve), continued commercial momentum (AMD), and explicit governance statements on superintelligence (Altman).
