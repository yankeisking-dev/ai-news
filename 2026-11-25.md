# AI News Roundup – November 25, 2026

A busy day in AI, dominated by corporate drama at Nvidia, bold research claims in math and science, and new agentic tools for coding and security. Here’s a clean summary of the top stories from the past ~24 hours.

### 1. Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO after Trump intervention
Nvidia’s board unanimously voted to bring back founder Jensen Huang as president, CEO, and director, effective immediately—just 11 days after he resigned amid reports of extramarital affairs (including one with a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly linked to Chinese intelligence). President Trump publicly claimed credit for convincing the board, and the company cited input from senior U.S. government officials that the return posed no national security risk. Shares surged ~32% on Monday (adding roughly $1 trillion in market value) before giving back some gains; Huang later told employees he was in therapy, apologized for past conduct, and expressed gratitude for a second chance. Critics (including Yale’s Jeffrey Sonnenfeld) have raised concerns about personal liability for directors.

### 2. Anthropic mathematician claims Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis
Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) released a Lean formalization he says proves the Riemann Hypothesis (and the Generalized RH for Dirichlet L-functions), developed over roughly six weeks with Claude. The proof is ~17 million lines across ~175k files; the Lean/Mathlib communities and independent checkers have certified that it compiles and matches the formal statement of the hypothesis. Alpöge has not yet published a human-readable paper (he described one generated version as exceeding 250,000 pages of “slop”). The claim remains under expert scrutiny.

### 3. OpenAI shows GPT-6 models can design proteins with lab validation
OpenAI published a preprint demonstrating that GPT-6 family models can design novel protein structures and sequences using only reasoning and general-purpose code (without specialized biology models generating the designs). In wet-lab tests with partner Onava, 35 of 322 minibinder designs (10.9%) showed binding across five targets (including PD-L1, VEGF-A, and IL-17A). Sequence redesign and antibody loop tasks also produced strong binding rates in computational and lab screens. Structure prediction remains a relative weakness compared with specialized tools.

### 4. Google DeepMind Gemini 4 agent teams cut quantum algorithm costs (Lean-verified)
Teams of Gemini 4 agents discovered more efficient quantum programs for arithmetic, fusion, photochemistry, and drug-metabolism problems. Every result was formally proved correct (and costed) in Lean, because the circuits are too large to simulate classically. Headline gains include a ~290× reduction for a fixed-point arcsin routine and roughly 11.8×, 8×, and 2.5× cuts for fusion, photochemistry, and cytochrome P450 simulations respectively. Agent teams shared results via a wiki and virtual conferences.

### 5. Anthropic launches Claude Ship for automated pull-request workflows
Claude Ship (beta for Team and Enterprise plans on GitHub) bundles three agents: Code Review (already generally available), PR Steward (handles failing CI, merge conflicts, and review feedback), and Claude Approvals (applies team-written policies to approve routine changes). Developers can also use a `/ship` command in Claude Code. The goal is to let teams “ship code as fast as they write it” while keeping humans in control of sensitive merges.

### 6. OpenAI and Cisco open-source Shirudo for live Linux protection without reboots
Shirudo (“shield” in Japanese) is an open-source eBPF-based runtime that lets operators (or AI agents) deploy temporary, signed “shields” that block exploit paths on running Linux systems while permanent patches are tested and rolled out. In controlled tests a Codex-generated shield stopped a Java4Shell exploit against Elasticsearch with no restart. The project is still pre-release and currently requires a specially patched kernel plus TPM 2.0.
