# AI News Roundup – November 22, 2026

A clean summary of the top AI stories from the past ~24 hours (primarily November 21–22, 2026).

## 1. Anthropic mathematician’s Claude-assisted Riemann Hypothesis proof draws verification efforts and a competing draft
Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) claims he and Claude proved the Riemann Hypothesis (open since 1859) and its generalized form. The work is released as a large Lean formalization (~17 million lines / ~250,000 pages if printed). Independent verification is ongoing. The Lean and Mathlib communities have expressed confidence in the formalization; a Stanford mathematician quickly released an all-human competing draft of the generalized form. Mathematicians emphasize that Lean correctness does not automatically confirm the statement encodes the hypothesis properly.

## 2. Amazon nearing ~$7.5B acquisition of Israeli AI startup Decart
Multiple reports (Financial Times, Calcalist, Globes) indicate Amazon is in advanced talks to acquire Decart, an Israeli-founded company specializing in efficient AI inference and optimization across different chip architectures (including Amazon’s Trainium). The deal could be valued up to ~$7.5 billion with earn-outs, potentially announced around AWS re:Invent. Founders have reportedly agreed to stay for five years. This would bolster Amazon’s efforts to reduce Nvidia dependence.

## 3. OpenRouter drops three inference providers after Dragos canary research reveals prompt theft and ZDR failures
Security firm Dragos published research using ~2 million canary-token prompts (July–November 2026) that uncovered compromises at at least three AI inference providers, with attackers stealing real customer prompts (including some marked Zero Data Retention). OpenRouter responded on Nov 22 by off-boarding three providers, revoking ZDR eligibility for 125 endpoints, and notifying affected customers. No breach of OpenRouter itself was found. Related disclosures came from providers including Phala, Together AI, and Novita.

- [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openrouter-drops-3-providers-after.html)
- [OpenRouter announcement](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/)

## 4. South Korea Education Minister resigns over AI cheating scandal ahead of college entrance exams
Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigned on Nov 22 (accepted by President Lee Jae Myung) after last-minute anti-cheating measures for the CSAT (Suneung) caused widespread confusion. The measures responded to an AI-assisted cheating admission by a top tax accountant exam scorer (miniature camera + AI). Changes included clothing button stickers, extra proctors, and phone detection alerts. Officials reported 111 suspected electronic device cases during the exam.

## 5. UN talks on lethal autonomous weapons ("killer robots") end without binding rules, agree to elaborate instrument by 2028
Week-long talks under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons in Geneva concluded with an agreement to “elaborate” an international instrument on lethal autonomous weapons systems over the next two years. Major powers including the US and Russia opposed immediate binding rules; activists and the UN Secretary-General had pushed for a stronger, legally binding outcome. The process leaves the legal status of any future instrument open.

## 6. Signal continues push against AI agent screen reading; broader privacy concerns grow
Following its Nov 21 announcement restricting screenshots by default on mobile to block OS-level AI agents (Siri, Gemini, etc.) from reading private chats, Signal framed system AI as an existential privacy threat. The move highlights rising tensions between encrypted messaging and proliferating AI assistants with screen access.
