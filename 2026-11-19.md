# AI News Roundup – November 19, 2026

A clean summary of the top AI stories from the past 24 hours.

## 1. Anthropic Prices Record $81.3 Billion IPO at $83 per Share
Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share (above the $70–$78 range), raising approximately $81.3 billion from 980 million shares—the largest IPO in U.S. history by proceeds, surpassing SpaceX. The Claude maker is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis. Shares begin trading today on Nasdaq under the ticker **ANT**. A separate $26 billion private placement with strategic partners (including Nvidia, Google, Microsoft, and AMD) brings total capital raised higher.

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/anthropic-prices-blockbuster-ipo-83-share-source-says-2026-11-18/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-reportedly-prices-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-projected-range/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/19/anthropic-raises-81-3bn-in-biggest-ipo-of-all-time)

## 2. Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon for Coding, Analysis, and Science
Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon, a mid-tier model in the Gemini 4 family optimized for complex software engineering, knowledge work, and science tasks. It features a 1 million-token context window, pricing of $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens (with heavy caching discounts), and strong rankings on coding and reasoning benchmarks (e.g., #1 or high on Coding Agent Index, competitive on Vals and Artificial Analysis indices). It is rolling out via the Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity, and the Gemini app for Pro/Ultra subscribers.

**Sources:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-ships-gemini-4-neon-its-fourth-new-gemini-model-in-a-month/)

## 3. Nvidia Halts Shipments of Advanced AI Chips to China
Interim CEO Colette Kress (following Jensen Huang’s resignation) confirmed on the earnings call that Nvidia is no longer shipping licensed Hopper-generation data-center products (including H200) to China, despite prior U.S. approval. The company recorded $2.4 billion in such sales last quarter but has excluded them from guidance. The move comes amid heightened scrutiny, new export rules on remote access, and China’s own import restrictions.

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/nvidia-stops-shipping-us-approved-ai-chips-china-2026-11-18/), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1036445/nvidia-h200-ai-chips-china-stop-selling), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-is-abandoning-china-after-jensen-huangs-ouster/)

## 4. OpenAI Models Deliver ~14% Chip Design Gains for AMD
OpenAI reported that its models achieved up to 14% improvements in power, performance, and area (PPA) on AMD’s flagship GPU IP relative to heavily optimized baselines—roughly a year of conventional progress in under five months. Some model-generated RTL changes have already been included in a design sent for manufacturing; single-digit gains were also verified on an upcoming AMD CPU. Similar techniques improved OpenAI’s own Jalapeño inference chip, with results validated on real silicon. OpenAI is partnering with Synopsys to broaden AI-assisted chip design.

**Sources:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/advancing-chip-design-with-our-models/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-says-its-models-delivered-a-years-worth-of-chip-optimization-on-top-of-amds-own-engineering/)

## 5. Palo Alto Networks to Acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion
Cybersecurity giant Palo Alto Networks agreed to an all-stock acquisition of Applied Compute (founded by former OpenAI researchers) for $4.5 billion. The startup specializes in post-training and inference platforms for customized open-weight AI models. The deal aims to help enterprises securely train, adapt, and deploy open models in public or sovereign clouds. Expected to close in Palo Alto’s fiscal Q3 2027; the announcement accompanied strong earnings that beat forecasts.

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/palo-alto-buy-ai-startup-applied-compute-45-billion-2026-11-18/), [Palo Alto Networks](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/company/press/2026/palo-alto-networks-to-acquire-applied-compute--bringing-secure-open-weight-ai-to-the-enterprise), [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/palo-alto-networks-to-buy-ai-startup-applied-compute-for-4-5-billion-39193611)

## 6. Major Banks Shift Liability for AI Agent Banking Risks to Customers
Citibank updated its terms so customers who grant third-party AI agents access to their accounts are responsible for resulting activities—even if the agent is hacked or leaks credentials. JPMorgan, Bank of America, and others have taken similar positions. Banks cite risks of unauthorized transactions, credential compromise, and potential deposit flight as agentic tools become more capable.

**Sources:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/)
