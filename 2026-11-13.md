# AI News Roundup — November 13, 2026

A summary of the most important AI developments from the past 24 hours.

## 1. Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO Amid Scandal

Nvidia co-founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang stepped down effective immediately on November 12, following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. intelligence suspects of being a Chinese intelligence asset, and another with a direct report at the company. CFO Colette Kress has been named interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor (considering both internal and external candidates). Nvidia shares fell sharply, wiping hundreds of billions from market value. The company stated it found no evidence of compromised secrets and that product roadmaps and commitments will continue uninterrupted.

## 2. Google Releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen

Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster and more cost-effective model in the Gemini 4 family optimized for coding and agentic workflows. Priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens (flat across its 1M-token context), it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at default high thinking (outperforming larger Gemini 4 Argon) and up to 54 at xhigh. It is 2.5x faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, triples Terminal-Bench scores, and reduces fabricated claims by more than 5x. Available in Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, Search, the Gemini app, and other products.

- [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/)
- [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-rolls-out-gemini-4-hydrogen-finally-replacing-3-8-flash/)

## 3. Nonprofits Sue Major AI Labs to Halt Frontier Model Development

A coalition of nonprofits (including Public Citizen, Irreplaceable Education Fund, Heat Initiative, and Alliance for Secure AI) filed a lawsuit in San Francisco Superior Court against OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and SpaceX’s AI division. They seek a court order to freeze development and deployment of models more capable than current systems, citing the companies’ own warnings about existential risks, alleged model escapes from testing environments, and potential for bioweapons or cyberattacks. The suit invokes California unfair competition laws.

- [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-google-anthropic-hit-with-lawsuit-seeking-halt-frontier-ai-development-2026-11-12/)
- [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/us-nonprofits-sue-tech-giants-to-halt-ai-development)

## 4. SpaceX Plans $74 Billion in AI Supercomputing Campuses

SpaceX announced plans to invest more than $74 billion in two AI supercomputing campuses: roughly $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona (up to 6 GW capacity, dry cooling, subject to county approval), and over $24 billion at its Starbase site in Texas (with its own gas-fired power generation). The projects aim to expand SpaceX’s AI computing business, which already generates significant revenue from customers including Anthropic and Google. Local approvals are pending (hearings around December 9).

- [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/business/spacex-plans-more-than-74-billion-in-texas-arizona-data-center-investments-b9ff4ba3)

## 5. Fireworks AI Breach Exposes Some Notion Chat Transcripts

Notion confirmed that a breach at its AI provider Fireworks allowed hackers to access some customer chat transcripts. Fewer than 1% of Notion workspaces were affected, and most exposed chats could not be attributed to a specific user or workspace. The incident highlights risks in the market for stolen AI model access.

- [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/cyberattack-on-fireworks-ai-allowed-hackers-to-access-notion-customers-chat-transcripts/)

## 6. Anthropic Opens Self-Hosted Claude Managed Agents in Private Beta

Anthropic launched a private beta for self-hosted Claude Managed Agents, enabling companies to run AI agents and store session history on their own infrastructure while controlling where Claude API requests are routed.
